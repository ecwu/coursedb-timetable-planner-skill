## Planning rules

Students must complete 18 GE units, normally six 3-unit courses:

| Level | Requirement | Units |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Level 1 Foundational | One course in each of Values and the Meaning of Life, Quantitative Reasoning, and History and Civilization | 9 |
| Level 2 Interdisciplinary Thematic | Two courses under one or two of Culture, Creativity and Innovation; Science, Technology and Society; and Sustainable Communities | 6 |
| Level 3 GE Capstone | One course under any one of Service-Learning, Service Leadership Education, Experiential Learning, or Interdisciplinary Independent Study | 3 |
| **Total** |  | **18** |

Important interpretation rules:

- Level 1 is three separate category requirements; a Level 1 course cannot substitute for another Level 1 category.
- Level 2 allows two courses from one theme or split across two themes. It does not require one course from all three themes.
- Level 3 requires one course from any one of the four capstone categories.
- The caret-marked categories on the source page are selection pools. The listed courses may be offered by other Faculties/School, with the GE Committee overseeing course planning and development.
- A major programme may decide to count a Level 3 project/course toward a major, minor, or concentration, but not toward Free Electives. If it is double-counted outside GE, the student must take another course to make up the required GE units or graduation units.
- Treat the catalogue below as candidate metadata. A course is usable for the student's term only after its exact code or known CourseDB variant has an Offering record for the target calendar term.

## Course-code usage

- These are the official base codes shown on the GE page. Query them as exact course codes first.
- If a concrete GE course is explicitly fixed by the Handbook or selected for the plan and CourseDB returns `NO_RECORD`, consult `course-code-expansion-map.md`. Use only a mapped concrete variant; never expand the entire GE catalogue or force a generic `8001`–`8009` expansion.
- For a student's topic preference, use the titles as a candidate interpretation, then verify the exact course's Offering, prerequisite text, and schedule. Do not present topic matching as a complete semantic search.
- A course listed under a GE category is a candidate for that category, not proof that it is offered in the target term or that the student's Handbook permits it in a different category.
